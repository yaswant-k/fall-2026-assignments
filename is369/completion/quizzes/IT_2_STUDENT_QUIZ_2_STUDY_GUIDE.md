## Core recall questions and checked answers
1. What is critical thinking according to the Delphi formulation? — Purposeful self-regulatory judgment involving interpretation, analysis, evaluation, and inference with reasoned explanation.
2. What three scaffolds did the authors use? — Authentic instruction, structured dialogue/role-play, and mentoring through a GPT-based Socratic peer tutor.
3. How does reflective use differ from thoughtless use? — Reflective users verify and revise AI advice; thoughtless users adopt without meaningful evaluation.
4. Does higher trust invariably improve AI collaboration? — No; trust may predict use while also encouraging ill-calibrated reliance.
5. What makes a solution creative? — Both novelty and usefulness.
6. Why can self-reported critical thinking fail to improve significantly while behavior changes? — Skill self-perception is a distinct measurement and the intervention was brief; do not overstate an untested mechanism.
7. How did direct adoption change? — The intervention reduced direct adoption of AI-generated material compared with the comparison group.
8. What is cosine similarity used for? — Comparing text representation vectors; lower similarity can indicate less overlap but does not by itself prove quality.
9. What are CPIDR and DEPID? — Automated, differently constructed estimates of propositional idea density, not full measures of creativity.
10. Why is binary appropriate/inappropriate reliance insufficient in ill-structured tasks? — No single verifiable outcome may exist; examine reasoning processes and multidimensional performance.
11. Name role-play stakeholders. — University administrators, instructors, students.
12. What is an important sample/duration limitation? — 226 students in one university context and a brief instructional intervention.
13. What should instructors do? — Use evidence evaluation, stakeholder dialogue, and reasoning prompts rather than assuming AI answers are adequate.
