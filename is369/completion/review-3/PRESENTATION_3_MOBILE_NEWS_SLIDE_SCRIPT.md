# Article Review 3
## Visual complexity and mobile news search
Yaswant Kakarla · IS 369 · Fall 2026
Guo et al. (2022), *Universal Access in the Information Society*
**Proposed article; approval not documented**
---
# Why publish this study?
- Small screens change the constraints of information search
- Interface quantity and interface layout are distinct design variables
- Objective eye tracking helps examine search patterns
- The problem affects mobile usability and satisfaction
---
# Purpose and design
**Question:** How do quantity complexity and layout complexity affect search behavior and satisfaction?

- Task-based controlled eye-tracking experiment
- **34 recruited, 30 analyzed**
- Controlled interface prototypes; subjective satisfaction measurement
- Primarily **quantitative** analysis; no interview themes asserted
---
# Who participated?
- **34 recruited; 30 completed and analyzed (16 male, 14 female)**
- Reported age range **21–26**; mean **23.85**, SD **1.42**
- Limited age and usage-profile diversity
---
# What was measured?
- **Fixation count:** periods of stable visual attention
- **Saccade count:** fast movements between visual targets
- **Revisit count:** repeated attention to areas
- **Satisfaction:** respondents' evaluation after tasks

These measures assess search patterns, not long-term comprehension.
---
# Results: quantity complexity
Higher quantity complexity was associated with:
- More fixations
- More saccades
- More revisits
- Lower satisfaction

**Design implication:** greater visible density can increase search effort.
---
# Results: layout complexity
- Revisit and saccade counts increased with layout complexity
- Fixation counts first fell, then rose
- Satisfaction had an **inverted-U** relationship with layout complexity

**Interpretation:** moderate structure may help; neither extreme is automatically best.
---
# Strengths, with supporting evidence
1. Separates amount of content from arrangement
2. Uses eye tracking, not only surveys
3. Compares systematically manipulated layouts
---
# Weaknesses, with supporting evidence
1. **n = 30 analyzed (34 recruited)**, limited age diversity
2. Experimental tasks do not fully reproduce everyday phone use
3. Two complexity variables cannot capture accessibility, ads, and personalization
---
# Future research
- Replicate with real phones, diverse ages, and accessibility needs
- Evaluate search accuracy and recall alongside eye movements
- Test contextual distractions and realistic personalized feeds
- Report confidence intervals and preregistered effect comparisons
---
# Review conclusion
**Useful empirical insight:** quantity and layout complexity have distinct relationships with search and satisfaction.

**Limit:** a controlled sample is not a universal product design rule.
---
# APA reference
